# Step 3 — Product Vision
*(Process notes — 2026-07-06)*

## Why this step existed
Before designing the site, we paused to envision the product itself. The brief says the device is conceptual, but a marketing site is only as convincing as the product behind it — and the required "design challenge" section lives entirely in the product's interaction design.

## The object
- **Form:** 38mm round pendant, 14g. Frosted translucent white glass dome in a matte warm-white ceramic body; one thin ring of warm metal around the glass — the only metal on the device.
- **Worn two ways:** thick woven loop (pendant) or magnetic clip (collar, lapel, bag strap). The same magnet docks it face-up on its charging dish at night.
- **Output:** small directional near-field speaker (talks softly toward you from your chest) + every exchange lands as text in the companion app.
- **Specs:** ~2-day battery, eSIM (works without the phone), zero screens/feeds/badges.

## Interaction model: the Trust Dial
Three listening postures, chosen by the user (this reframed the "hold-to-talk vs wake word" decision from a compromise into the signature feature):
1. **Muted** — twist the metal ring a quarter-turn; microphones physically disconnect. The misaligned notch is visible across a table: muted is a state other people can verify.
2. **Touch (default)** — hold the glass to talk, release to stop. It cannot listen unless your fingers are on it.
3. **Open ear (opt-in)** — wake word "Amber?" for hands-busy moments; the glass holds a visible amber wash the entire time the mic is open.

## The light language (design challenge #1 candidate → chosen)
No screen, so the glass says everything:
- **Idle:** plain frosted white — no light, cannot hear.
- **Listening:** amber light fills the glass, breathing (~4s cycle).
- **Thinking:** a thin amber arc orbits the rim.
- **Speaking:** the rim ring pulses with each word.
- **Open ear:** steady low amber wash (visible to bystanders).
- **Muted:** ring twisted, notch misaligned, glass white.

**The governing rule: "The light never lies — if Amber can hear, it glows."** Lamp and microphone share one power path, so glow and hearing cannot disagree. This single rule answers both candidate design challenges (state-without-a-screen AND bystander trust) at once.

Iterations: v1 had a glowing core dot filling the dark face → feedback: face should be white translucent when inactive, amber fills when active, ring-spin for thinking/speaking → v2 white-glass system above. Bezel slimmed (~12% → 6.5% of diameter), then metal bezel replaced by ceramic body + thin mute ring after Pinterest board review.

## Pinterest board influence (pinterest.com/raysums/ai-device)
Board themes read from 12 pins: round pebble forms on thick soft cords; matte ceramic/fabric materials, almost no polished metal or chains; neutrals with one confident accent color; intimate worn-on-clothing photography; tactile dials.
Changes made in response: aluminum bezel → matte ceramic body with a single thin metal mute ring; "chain" → thick woven loop; photography direction for the site: product-on-body closeups, soft studio neutrals.

## Hero use cases (chosen from six candidate territories)
1. **Absorb (message triage)** — reachable, never interruptible; Amber takes the pings and speaks up only for what's personally urgent.
2. **Keep (capture & memory)** — thoughts, todos, commitments caught in the moment, searchable in the vault; the name's own metaphor.
3. **Bookend (presence rituals)** — spoken morning brief, evening recap when docked; the day starts and ends with a voice, not a screen. Feeds the site's Dusk chapter.
Deliberately demoted: Q&A utility (table stakes — shown in scenarios, never led with); think-out-loud companionship (hard to demo credibly). **Accessibility woven through all three** (everything eyes-free/hands-free by design) rather than used as a marketing pillar.

## Scenarios written (site-ready)
Morning walk without the phone (absorb) · visibly-muted meeting, recall afterwards (keep) · cooking with open ear (utility) · docking on the dish at night (bookend).
