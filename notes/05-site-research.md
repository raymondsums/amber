# Step 5 — Pre-build Research: the Reference Bar & Site Patterns
*(Process notes — 2026-07-06. Two research passes before building: the brief's cited reference, and a teardown of best-in-class hardware sites.)*

## A. The cited reference: AREA 17 × OpenAI
Key findings from area17.com/clients/openai, their archive, and the brand-guidelines PDF coverage:
- Case study is structured by **outcome claims, not process phases** — four gerund headlines ("Expanding… Strengthening… Putting… Building") that narrate the whole story on their own; reasoning lives inside 120–200-word sections; deliverables appear only as a terse end-of-page taxonomy.
- One **governing brand idea** ("OpenAI is an empty vessel that adapts to its content") visibly decides everything: 4-style type ramp (Signifier serif + Söhne sans/mono), black-and-white base + 30 optional content-driven color themes, warm candid portraits instead of tech tropes.
- Craft-depth signaled by **one obsessive detail** (icon stroke weights paired to type weights), not exhaustive screens.
- **Applied to our final writeup:** structure the project walkthrough as 4 outcome claims; our governing idea = "calm you can verify" (warmth without glare / the light never lies); our obsessive detail = the mic-lamp shared power path; end with receipts (live site + Figma + guidelines).

## B. Hardware-site teardown (Apple, Nothing, Teenage Engineering, Daylight)
Build rules adopted:
1. One idea per viewport; ≤ ~10 sections per page (hero → tiles → 2–3 chapters → trust → CTA).
2. Headlines 3–8 words, sentence case, period. Body ≤ 40 words/block.
3. Product render carries the viewport; alternate isolated-on-neutral with one or two warm lifestyle shots.
4. No spec table on the homepage; Product Details ends with a TE-style plain-text spec list under category headers (precision as premium signal).
5. One CTA phrase everywhere — "Reserve yours" — nav + hero + page end; price exactly once (Product Details), paired with an availability line, not urgency.
6. Apple product-subnav pattern: wordmark left, three tabs, CTA right. Three pages = three tabs.
7. How It Works = chaptered deep-dive, not a manual; ends with the trust block — the site's ONE long sincere passage (~120 words), per Daylight's founder-note move.
8. Restraint budgets: ≤ 1 animation/section, accent only where Amber acts, no auto-carousels, air between sections larger than their padding.

## C. Build decisions that followed
- Real static site at `~/ai/a1-amber/site/` (3 pages + shared CSS/JS + fonts + img), previewable on localhost, deployable to Vercel for the submission's live link.
- Lifestyle photography generated by Ray via Nano Banana against an 8-shot art-directed list (consistency block + per-shot prompts, golden-hour domestic, device close-ups); site built with graceful placeholders that swap in as images land.
- Price point set at **$249, no subscription** — inside OpenAI's reported $200–300 "right" range, far from Humane's fatal $699+$24/mo; availability line "Ships early 2027."
