## Five cross-cutting findings
1. **"Presence" is the category's shared emotional promise, not a differentiator.** Plaud: "Be fully present." Humane: "stay present," "moments, not minutes." Meta: "without missing out." Everyone fights divided attention. Presence can be our emotional close, but it cannot be the ownable claim.
2. **The "natural interface / conversation-first" position is unclaimed.** No site claims to be the natural *form* for AI itself. Meta comes closest functionally ("Ask your glasses anything") but sells fashion. → Amber's hero ("The most natural way to talk to AI") sits in genuinely open space. Verified.
3. **Nobody pairs presence with proof.** The category says "trust us" (policy language, badges, or silence). No one makes a *hardware* honesty claim. "The light never lies / private by physics" remains unclaimed. Verified.
4. **"No subscription" graduated to headline-level copy post-Humane** (Rabbit puts it beside the price; Limitless: "No subscription required."). Humane hid its $24/mo in a footnote and died partly for it. → Keep ours explicit at both CTA bands; price + "no subscription" always adjacent.
5. **Humane's cautionary lesson is about copy, not just product: it sold a philosophy without a job-to-be-done** — no concrete "why this instead of your phone." → Our structure (philosophy hero + three concrete jobs + scenario dialogue) is the direct antidote; keep both layers.

## Verdict on Amber's current homepage copy (v3)
- Hero claim: **keep** — unique in the field, and the only claim that explains the form factor.
- "Be present again." final CTA: **keep as emotional close** — shared category language is fine at the close; differentiation happens above it.
- "Private by physics" coda: **keep, low placement correct** — consumer plays bury privacy, pro plays lead with it; a proof-backed coda threads the needle.
- Concrete jobs + scenario dialogue: **keep** — the anti-Humane insurance.
- No copy collisions found with any live competitor headline.
