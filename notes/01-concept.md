# Step 1 — Concept & Positioning
*(Process notes for the walkthrough — captured 2026-07-06)*

## The brief, decoded
Design a brand identity + marketing website for a conceptual AI device/companion (voice + text, goes everywhere with you). The device itself is a narrative vehicle; the deliverables are the brand system and the 3-screen site (Homepage, Product Details, Features/How It Works). Reference bar: AREA 17's OpenAI work (editorial, story-driven).

## Clarifications from the team (via email)
- Brief is intentionally open-ended; they want to see how I define product vision, target users, and the problem. **Assumptions must be stated explicitly with rationale.**
- Assume a **global audience** — identity and UX should resonate broadly, not one market.
- **A1 is a standalone brand** — do not align with BJAK's existing visual language.
- AI tools are allowed; what matters is the thinking and how decisions are reached.

## Concept: Amber, by A1

**Product:** Amber — a small wearable pendant/clip AI companion. It listens when invited, speaks in your ear or aloud, and continues conversations as text in its companion app. No screen on the device; a soft amber light is its only face.

**Positioning:** Calm technology. Your phone demands attention; Amber absorbs it. It handles pings, reminders, questions, and half-thoughts so you can stay present in the room you're actually in.

**Target user:** People who feel their phone has colonized their attention — busy professionals, parents, creatives. Not gadget collectors; people who'd rather look at a sunset than a lock screen.

**Name rationale:** Amber is (1) a warm glow — light without glare, the calm-tech promise; (2) a pendant stone — the form factor; (3) a preserver of memories — what the assistant does with your day; (4) a human name, so speaking to it feels natural; (5) starts with A, creating a family logic with the A1 house brand. Globally pronounceable, no negative meanings found in major languages.

**Brand architecture:** A1 is the company (standalone, not BJAK-derived); Amber is its first product. The site is A1's product site for Amber — the same way the OpenAI site presents the company through its products.

**One-liner candidates:** "Stay in the moment." / "Technology that gives your attention back." / "The warmth of tech, without the glare."

## Assumptions log (running)
1. The device is a screenless wearable pendant/clip (brief allows any form). Chosen because it makes the strongest case for voice+text interaction and creates the richest HCI story.
2. A1 = house brand / company; Amber = product name. The brief asks to promote the companion; a named product with a house brand mirrors how credible hardware brands launch (Nothing → Ear, Teenage Engineering → OP-1).
3. Global audience → English-first site with plain, translation-friendly copy; color and symbolism vetted for broad cultural neutrality; accessibility to WCAG AA.
4. Price point / availability: positioned as a premium pre-order product (lets the site have a real conversion goal: reserve/waitlist — gives the "business impact" section teeth).

## Design challenge candidates (pick 1 later)
- **A. State without a screen:** how a screenless device communicates listening / thinking / muted / done — light language + haptics, taught interactively by the website itself.
- **B. Social trust:** how a wearable mic signals to *other people in the room* that it isn't recording — hardware mute, outward-facing cues.
